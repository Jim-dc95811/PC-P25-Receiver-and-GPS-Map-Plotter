This document is a concept and architecture overview prepared as an engineering proposal. It describes a working, bench-proven capability and the path that produced it. It is not an official product specification and uses no proprietary or agency-specific data.

1Executive Summary

Every fielded P25 radio already transmits its own position in a standardized, open format. Today that information is only usable by the agency that owns the radio system, and only through that system's own infrastructure. Universal Traveler recovers it with nothing but a receiver.

The system takes the recovered baseband at an ordinary analog receiver's detector output, decodes the standardized P25 Tier-1 location data (TIA-102.BAJB) entirely in software, and plots every unit on an offline map by its individual ID — with no SDR, no cooperating base station, no network, and no dependence on the fleet's infrastructure. It runs on commodity hardware: an ordinary PC, or a self-contained embedded appliance built on a sub-$100 router. The display is any web browser on any device.

The result closes a real and structural gap. Vendor location services map an agency's own units through the agency's own network. Open-source monitors decode P25 off the air but require an SDR and produce no location product. Nothing on the market delivers standards-based location, passively, off the air, on commodity hardware. This proposal documents that capability, the architecture behind it, and the development history that proved it end to end.

3How We Got Here

The architecture was not designed on paper and then built. It was proven one checkpoint at a time, each validated against ground truth before the next was attempted. The path is the strongest argument for the design.

  • The insightDetector audio is already demodulated. An FM receiver tuned to a P25 channel recovers the four-level C4FM baseband at its discriminator/detector output. The hard RF work is already done in the radio — so no SDR and no re-demodulation are required. Tap that output into a sound card and the signal is digital symbols waiting to be sliced.
  • The decode chainFrom baseband to a standardized sentence. A faithful software chain was assembled: automatic gain control and a matched symbol filter, a minimum-mean-square-error loop that recovers symbol clock and level to a clean eye, 48-bit frame synchronization, network-ID decode (BCH), data-PDU assembly, and rate-1/2 convolutional error correction decoded with a Viterbi algorithm. The output is the standardized Tier-1 payload: an NMEA position plus the transmitting radio's individual ID.
  • Ground-truth proofByte-for-byte against the answer key. The decoded sentence was compared, character for character, against the position the cooperating base independently reported for the same transmission. On a clean capture the match was exact on every burst — position, altitude, checksum, and unit ID. Correctness was measured, not asserted.
  • Live off the airFirst over-the-air decode. With a radio on the bench, the system decoded a live transmission the instant the unit keyed up — full position and unit ID, from the sound card, with no base station and no SDR in the path. The capability was proven on the real channel, not only on recordings.
  • DeploymentCommodity hardware, offline map. The same decoder was packaged two ways: as a pure-PC application, and as a self-contained embedded appliance on a sub-$100 router. Each serves an offline slippy map that any browser opens — units plotted by ID, with live / stale / alert states and an operator's own position — with nothing installed on the viewing device and no internet required.
  • HardeningMargin at the edge. A soft-decision upgrade to the error-correction stage — carrying the demodulator's analog confidence into the decoder instead of discarding it — yields roughly 3 dB of coding gain in simulation, extending usable range exactly where the weakest unit is about to drop off the map. A flat line-level front end removes capture-side distortion, a separate and additive improvement.
  • ExtensionLocation and voice, fused by ID. Because the same stream carries both position and voice, a licensed vocoder lets the system add monitored audio — with the current speaker identified from the voice frame's own source address and highlighted on the map. Position and voice are joined by the one key they share: the unit ID.

4System Architecture

The system is a clean layered pipeline. Each layer has one job and a defined interface to the next; the proven signal-processing core is a sealed module, and everything around it is standard plumbing.

Figure 1 — Layered architecture. The decode engine (amber) is the proven core; the RF, fusion, and presentation layers are standard interfaces around it. The same engine ships as a PC application or a self-contained appliance.

4.1  RF / Front-End Layer

An ordinary wideband analog receiver on the target channel, tapped at its flat detector/discriminator output and carried into a commodity audio interface (a USB sound card, or a line-level interface for best fidelity). No SDR, no RF design, no modification to any transmitting radio. The system is strictly receive-only.

4.2  Decode Engine (the sealed core)

The recovered baseband is turned into standardized location data entirely in software: gain control and matched filtering, MMSE symbol-clock and level recovery, frame synchronization, network-ID decode, data-PDU assembly, and soft-decision convolutional error correction, yielding the Tier-1 NMEA position and the source individual ID. An optional branch extracts the voice frames and routes them to a licensed AMBE+2 vocoder for monitored audio. This layer is validated to the bit and treated as a fixed module.

4.3  Fusion Layer

Every report is filed under the transmitting radio's individual ID. Position reports and voice activity for the same unit are joined on that single key, which is why a talking unit's marker can light up on the map the instant it keys. Per-unit state (live, stale, alert on prolonged silence) is maintained here.

4.4  Presentation Layer

An offline map server renders units over the operator's own imagery — no cloud, no cellular, usable in a dead zone — with each unit plotted by ID, an incident-commander overview, the operator's own position, and (with the voice option) a current-speaker indicator and audio level. The display is any web browser on any device; nothing is installed on the viewer.

6Operating Principles

  • Receive-only, always. The system never transmits and never alters a radio. It listens.
  • Validate against ground truth. Decode correctness is measured by diffing against an independent reference, not assumed from "it looks right."
  • Protect the operator at the margin. Monitored audio is quality-gated before the vocoder: marginal frames are muted rather than played as artifacts, the call is held patiently through brief fades, and the threshold is conservative but tunable. Marginal signals are normal; the system degrades gracefully instead of failing loudly.
  • One coherent build. Proven algorithm kernels are sealed and reused; the plumbing around them is purpose-built, not patched together.

7Honest Boundaries & Risks

  • It is a receiver. It decodes what it adequately receives; coverage is bounded by RF reality. The soft-decision margin extends the edge — it does not resurrect a truly dead signal.
  • Not a replacement where infrastructure exists. Where an agency runs a full system AVL, that remains the authoritative source. Universal Traveler's value is the infrastructure-free, disconnected, and universal cases — and as an independent cross-check.
  • Encryption is respected. The system reads the standardized location data present in the clear; it is not a cipher-breaking tool and makes no such claim.
  • Deployment is policy-governed. Passive monitoring of location is a capability with clear operational value for an agency's own personnel safety and equally clear policy considerations; governance is a deployment decision, called out here deliberately rather than buried.

8Status & Roadmap

Proven today, end to end: live over-the-air Tier-1 decode on commodity hardware; the offline map plotter on both a PC and a self-contained appliance; the soft-decision decoder, built and simulation-verified. In progress: field measurement of the soft-decision margin against a calibrated reference; integration of the licensed-vocoder voice option with the current-speaker display. Productization path: package the appliance build, formalize the front-end bill of materials, and define the operator-facing configuration.
